The iPhone can't do this by itself. Its limits are about time: how long, or between which hours. To lock apps until a task is done you need an app that joins your to-do list to Screen Time, and that's what Veggies First is.

## What the iPhone does by itself

You can [set a daily time allowance in Screen Time](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-schedules-and-time-allowances-iphb0c7313c9/ios) for the kinds of apps you scroll. That's a budget. It doesn't know whether you've done the laundry, and it runs down just as fast when you're scrolling to avoid the thing you meant to do.

Veggies First asks a different question. It doesn't ask how long you've been on the app. It asks what you've done today.

## How Veggies First locks apps behind your list

You make two lists. Your to-dos for the day are "veggies". The apps you want locked are "dessert": pick single apps, whole categories or websites.

![The lock screen on a dessert app: locked until you finish a veggie, with today's veggies listed.](/img/02-locked.jpg)

- **Open a locked app and you see your list.** The lock screen shows what's left to do, with the things you've marked as your focus first.
- **Each to-do you finish earns time.** One coin (DDC in the app) is one minute, and you choose how many coins a veggie pays.
- **Finish everything and the lock is gone** for the rest of the day.

Newly added apps lock right away. There's nothing to schedule.

## "I'll just take the app off the list"

That's the first thing everyone tries. An app you remove from dessert stays locked until your plate is clean or midnight, whichever comes first, so a lock can't be dodged in the moment.

Deleting a to-do to make the list shorter doesn't work either. Deleting one you're not getting to counts against today, so the plate isn't clean. A to-do you typed by mistake comes off without counting, a few times a day.

## What if the list is empty?

Then there'd be nothing to finish, and nothing would ever lock. So a day that starts with an empty plate starts locked, until you add a veggie or tell the lock screen you have nothing to do today. Planning tomorrow's list tonight takes a minute and avoids it.

## When you need in anyway

There are passes for that: a small number a day that open dessert without finishing anything. They take a hold of a few seconds to use, on purpose. More on how the whole thing fits together in [How Veggies First works](/guides/how-veggies-first-works/).

## Try it

Pick the two or three apps you'd most like to earn, put three things on today's list, and see how the first locked screen feels. Veggies First is free to try for 14 days.
