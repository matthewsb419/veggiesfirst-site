The person most likely to beat your app blocker is you, late in the day, with the phone in your hand. A blocker you can switch off in two taps is only as strong as you are at that moment. Veggies First was built by trying every way round it and then closing each one.

Here are the usual moves, and what happens when you try them.

## "I'll just change the settings"

Make the session longer, the break shorter, the reward bigger. You can, but changes to those rules start at midnight. Tonight's rules were set by this morning's you, and they stay.

## "I'll take the app off the list"

An app you remove from your dessert apps stays locked until your plate is clean or midnight, whichever comes first.

## "I'll delete the to-do"

Deleting a to-do you haven't done counts against today, so the plate can't be clean and dessert stays on the meter. A genuine mistake, like something typed twice, comes off without counting, but only a few times a day.

## "I'll push it to tomorrow"

Moving a to-do to tomorrow only unlocks at your evening review time. In the morning the choices are to do it, edit it or delete it. And one you push off still counts against today.

## "I'll leave the list empty"

A day that starts with nothing planned starts locked, with no passes, until you add something or tell the lock screen you have nothing to do.

## "I'll use a pass. And another."

Passes are allowed. They're the pressure valve. But using one takes a hold of several seconds, and if you let go early it starts over. After one pass, the next needs a finished to-do first. And the pass plan can only be changed on a day you haven't used one yet.

## "I'll just tick it off without doing it"

For this one you can bring in another person. Turn on proof and a to-do asks for a photo, a short video or a note when you finish it. A friend looks and decides whether it counts, and part of the coins wait for their answer.

## What it won't do

It won't trap you. If you delete the app, the locks come off. If a paid plan ends, your apps open; the app never keeps them locked when you aren't paying. This is a commitment you make with yourself, and you can always leave it.

Everything above also sits beside real flexibility: days off, passes, a big-day reward for getting a lot done. The rules give way when you've planned ahead for it, and hold when you try to change them in the moment.
