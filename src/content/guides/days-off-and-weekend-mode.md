A system with no days off breaks the first time you get sick or go to a wedding. One with unlimited days off isn't a system. What works is in between: rest you decide on ahead of time. Veggies First calls it Weekend mode.

## What Weekend mode is

A Weekend mode day needs no to-dos. The apps you'd normally have to earn are open all day, with no sessions and no breaks.

You get a set number a week, and they don't have to fall on the weekend. Use one on a Wednesday if that's your day.

## You pick it the day before

You switch on "Weekend mode tomorrow" the day before. You can't wake up, look at a full list and declare a holiday.

That one rule separates a rest day from a bad day. Tonight you can judge fairly whether tomorrow should be off. Tomorrow morning you'd say yes every time.

## You can cancel it, once

If the day turns out to be a good one for getting things done, tap "Turn off Weekend mode for today". It becomes a normal day and you get the day off back to use later. Once it's turned off for a day it can't be turned back on for that day, so you can't flip back and forth.

## It doesn't count against you

If you keep a goal for clearing your plate so many days a week, Weekend mode days don't count for or against it. Neither do days with nothing planned. A planned rest never shows up as a failure in your history.

## Other kinds of slack

Weekend mode is for whole days. The app has smaller ones too:

- **Passes** open your apps for a while without finishing a to-do. See [Passes: for the days you need your apps anyway](/guides/passes/).
- **The evening open window** opens them for an hour or two each night at a time you set.
- **An easy day.** If today's list is empty, the lock screen lets you say "I have nothing to do, life is easy", and dessert opens. Add a to-do later and the normal lock is back.

## Earn an extra one

Have a big day, where you finish far more than you usually do, and it banks a rest pass: one more Weekend day to use once the week's own are gone. Hard days pay for easy ones.

## Why this matters

People don't usually quit a habit on a hard day. They quit after a day that slipped, when the record looks spoiled and it's easy to think you've blown it and might as well stop. A day off that's part of the rules never gives you that excuse. You rested, on purpose, and tomorrow's plate is already set.
