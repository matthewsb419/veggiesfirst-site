The best time to write a to-do list is the evening before. You know what didn't get done and what's coming, and you aren't yet tired of the day you're planning. Two minutes at night means the morning starts with a decision already made.

Veggies First has this built in as the evening review.

## How the review goes

At a time you choose, a notification asks you to review the day. Open the app and it comes up by itself, in two pages.

**Page one: what's left.** Each unfinished veggie gets a choice: Done, Tomorrow, Backlog, or Let it go. Every leftover gets a decision, so nothing just sits there making you feel bad.

**Page two: tomorrow's plate.** It shows what's already on tomorrow, including repeats, with your backlog to pick from. Add what's missing and confirm.

At midnight, tomorrow's plate becomes today's.

![A plate in Veggies First: the day's to-dos in the order they'll be done.](/img/01-plate.jpg)

## Why moving things waits until evening

During the day, you can't swipe a to-do to tomorrow. That move only unlocks at your review time. Before then the choices are to do it, edit it or delete it.

It sounds strict, and it's one of the most useful rules in the app. At 9 AM, "I'll do it tomorrow" is how you avoid the thing. At 8 PM it's an honest call about a day that's nearly over.

Anything you do push off still counts against today, so the plate isn't clean. That keeps you from planning ten things knowing you'll bump seven.

## The backlog is for someday

Not everything belongs on a day. The backlog holds the things you'll get to sometime, and a tap puts one on tomorrow's plate when you're ready. It keeps your daily list short without losing anything.

## If you skip it

Reminders go off while tomorrow's plate is still empty. If you ignore those too, tomorrow starts locked until you add something. The app would rather you plan badly than not at all.

## Planning further out

A calendar takes to-dos for any day ahead, next week or next semester. There's also a look at the next seven plates with their repeats, where you can finish something early if you're on a roll.

## Try it

Pick a review time a little before you usually wind down, and keep the first few lists short: three things you'll really do. Veggies First is free to try for 14 days.
