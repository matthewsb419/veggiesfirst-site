A limit says "you've had enough". Earning says "you've done something, go ahead". The second one is easier to live with, because the scrolling stops being something you sneak and becomes something you've paid for.

Veggies First runs on that idea. Here's what it looks like in a normal day.

## The exchange rate

Every to-do you finish (a "veggie") pays coins. The app calls them DDC, for Dopamine Dessert Coins. One coin buys one minute in the apps you've marked as dessert, and it's counted as minutes you really use, shared across all of them.

You set the rate. How many coins a veggie pays is a slider, and so is how long one sitting can run.

![Today's plate in Veggies First, with the coins earned so far.](/img/01-plate.jpg)

## Some things are worth more

A gym trip and taking out the trash aren't the same effort. Turn on per-task rewards and each veggie gets a size: small pays half, standard pays the usual, big pays double, or you type your own number.

One tip that's built into the app's own advice: reward the dull stuff. Paying yourself for things you already enjoy can make them feel like chores.

## You can't binge the balance

Coins are spent in sessions. When a session is used up, dessert takes a break before the next one starts, even if you have coins left. Banked coins start the next session once the break ends. With none left, dessert stays locked until you finish another veggie.

That break is the point. It's the stopping place a feed doesn't have.

## Coins don't pile up for the weekend

At midnight, every coin you didn't spend drops into a piggy bank. It never comes back as dessert time. You can't work hard on Monday and scroll all of Saturday on the proceeds. Each day earns its own.

The piggy bank isn't wasted, though. It pays for an optional farm game inside the app.

## The big payout

Finish every veggie on the day's plate and the meter comes off: dessert is open for the rest of the day. That's the real prize, and it's why a short, honest list beats a long ambitious one.

## And if you check one off that you didn't do?

You can, the same as you can lie to any to-do list. If that's a worry, the app has an answer: proof. A veggie can ask for a photo, a short video or a note, and a friend decides whether it counts. Part of its coins wait for their verdict.

## Try it

Start with the standard reward for everything and one honest list. Veggies First is free to try for 14 days, which is long enough to find your own rate. The basics are in [How Veggies First works](/guides/how-veggies-first-works/).
