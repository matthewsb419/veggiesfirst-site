"Spend less time on my phone" is one of the most common resolutions, and it rarely lasts. It names an amount and has no rule behind it, so nothing happens on the evening you ignore it. A resolution that lasts is one the phone can hold you to.

Here's one: to-dos first, then your apps.

## Make it a rule the phone keeps

Veggies First joins a to-do list to your screen time. You put the day's to-dos on a plate and mark the apps you scroll as dessert. Then:

- while a to-do is left, those apps show your list in place of a feed,
- each one you finish earns minutes,
- and a clean plate opens them for the rest of the day.

You haven't promised to scroll less. You've set things up so the scrolling comes after the things you care about. The "less" tends to follow.

## Set it up while you still mean it

The turn of the year is when you're most sure. Use that. The rules you set (what a to-do earns, how long a sitting runs) can only be changed from the next midnight, and an app you take off the list stays locked for the rest of the day. The March version of you can't undo it at 11 PM.

## Plan for the bad days now

Resolutions usually end on the first day that goes wrong. So give those days a place in the rules:

- **Passes** for when you need your apps anyway.
- **Weekend mode** for a real day off, picked the day before. More in [How to take a day off without wrecking your habits](/guides/days-off-and-weekend-mode/).
- **Big days**, which reward doing far more than usual even if one thing got missed.

A day that bends the rules is still a day inside them.

## Keep a score you can stand to look at

Set a goal, like under two hours a day in your dessert apps, and the app shows how many of the last four weeks' days you made it, with a calendar. It leads with the good days. "22 of 28" in February is a resolution that's working, whatever happened on the other six. See [Screen time goals](/guides/goals-and-streaks/).

## Start smaller than you want to

Two or three apps and three to-dos a day. A January you can keep is worth more than a perfect first week.

## The built-in option

You can also [set a daily time allowance in Screen Time](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-schedules-and-time-allowances-iphb0c7313c9/ios). If a plain cap is all you need, start there. If you've tried that before and found yourself tapping past it by the second week, a rule tied to what you've done is the next thing to try.

## Try it

Set it up today, in ten minutes: your dessert apps, three things for tomorrow, and an evening review time. Veggies First is free to try for 14 days, which gets you well into January. The full picture is in [How Veggies First works](/guides/how-veggies-first-works/).
