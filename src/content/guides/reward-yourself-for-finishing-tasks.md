A reward system for yourself usually fails in one of two ways. You forget to give the reward, or you take it before the work. For it to work, the reward has to arrive right after the task, and it has to be out of reach until then.

Your phone already holds the reward you want most, several times an hour. Veggies First puts it behind your to-do list.

## The reward is the scrolling you'd do anyway

You don't have to invent treats or keep a chart. The apps you'd open regardless become the payout. Mark them as dessert, and while a to-do is left they show your list in place of a feed.

Finish a to-do and you earn coins. A coin is a minute in those apps. The reward comes the moment you tick the box, and you can't get it sooner.

![Today's plate in Veggies First, with coins earned for the to-dos that are done.](/img/01-plate.jpg)

## Size the reward to the task

Set every to-do to pay the same and it just works. Or turn on per-task rewards and give each a size: small pays half, standard is standard, big pays double, and custom is any number you like.

A useful rule from the app's own tips: reward the dull stuff. Paying yourself for things you already like doing can turn them into chores. Save the big payouts for the tax form and the cold-weather run.

## Small rewards, then a large one

Each to-do pays a few minutes. Finishing the whole list pays the big one: your apps are open for the rest of the day with no meter. So there's something to work for at every step, and a reason to finish the last one.

On days when you do far more than usual, there's a bonus on top. That's in [Big days](/guides/big-days/).

## Things that reward themselves

Some to-dos can be counted by the phone:

- **A step goal** finishes itself when you hit the number, and on a day with one, extra miles keep paying.
- **A place goal** pays by the minute you spend somewhere, like the gym or the library, up to the time you set.

You don't tick those. You do them, and the coins arrive.

## A few more small rewards

Finishing things also fills in a collection of vegetable badges, each with facts and recipes, and unspent coins go to a piggy bank that pays for an optional farm game. None of that unlocks your apps. It's extra.

## Why not just promise yourself a treat?

Because nothing holds you to the promise. A reward you can take at any time doesn't work as a reward. This arrangement works because the phone keeps the treat until the work is done, and hands it over the moment it is.
