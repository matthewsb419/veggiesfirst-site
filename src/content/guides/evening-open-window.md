Any system that locks your apps until the list is done has one obvious weak spot: the day you wrote too long a list. If the only choices are to finish everything or get nothing, you'll start writing shorter and shorter lists. The evening open window is Veggies First's answer.

## What it does

Every night, from a time you pick, your dessert apps open for one hour or two, whether the plate is clean or not. When the window closes, the lock goes back on whatever's still left.

It's the reverse of the usual downtime setting. That blocks your apps for a stretch of the evening. This one makes sure you get a stretch of the evening with them open.

It's off until you turn it on.

## Set it by day

One time for every night is the simple version. If your week isn't that even, you can give each day its own: drag a day's block up or down on a week calendar, or tap the day and set the exact time and length. A day can be closed altogether.

You can copy one day to all the weekdays or to the weekend. So 8 to 9 on work nights and 9 to 11 on Friday and Saturday takes about four taps.

## What it doesn't do

It doesn't pretend the list got done.

- What's left still counts for today. There's no clean-sweep bonus.
- It's all still there in the evening review, waiting for a decision.
- If you keep a goal for clearing your plate, the day isn't marked as cleared.

It also ends by 11:59 PM. Midnight brings tomorrow's plate and tomorrow's rules.

So the window gives you your evening. It doesn't give you credit.

## The earned version

If you'd rather your free evening came from effort than from the clock, there's a companion to it. On a big day, where you finish far more than usual, "Call it a day" opens dessert until midnight and moves what's left to tomorrow. Some people run both. Others turn the window off and rely on big days.

## Who it's for

- People who always plan a bit too much and don't want to stop.
- Anyone with one fixed thing in the evening, like a show or a call with friends, that shouldn't hang on whether the laundry's done.
- Parents who want a kid's phone to have a set free hour.

## Who should leave it off

If the lock is the only thing getting your list finished, a guaranteed window may take the edge off it. Try a week without one first. Passes and days off cover the odd exception.

## Try it

Start with one hour, later than you think you need, and move it earlier if the evenings feel tight. Veggies First is free to try for 14 days. For whole days off, see [How to take a day off without wrecking your habits](/guides/days-off-and-weekend-mode/).
